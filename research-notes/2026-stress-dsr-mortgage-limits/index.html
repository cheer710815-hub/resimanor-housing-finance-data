South Korea 2026 Stress DSR Mortgage Limits: A Reproducible Reference Note

Resimanor Housing Finance Research Note No. 1
Author: Eun Kim · Affiliation: Resimanor · ORCID: 0009-0006-9445-4768 · Release date: 2026-10-01

Working paper / research note. This note is not peer reviewed.

Abstract

This research note documents a reproducible reference framework for comparing mortgage borrowing capacity in South Korea under the 2026 Stress Debt Service Ratio (Stress DSR) regime. It explains how annual income, standardized debt-service assumptions, and existing credit-loan obligations interact when constructing mortgage-limit scenarios. The objective is not to reproduce an individual bank's underwriting decision, but to provide a transparent benchmark for research, education, and housing-finance analysis.

1. Research question

How does estimated mortgage borrowing capacity change when annual income changes or when a borrower already has unsecured credit debt, holding the remaining analytical assumptions constant?

2. Scope and interpretation

The note uses standardized reference scenarios. It is therefore a comparative research aid, not a loan approval calculator. Actual lending decisions may differ because financial institutions apply their own interest rates, maturity assumptions, repayment methods, credit policies, regulatory interpretations, and borrower-specific information.

3. Data and reproducibility

The underlying dataset contains income-based mortgage-limit examples and scenarios showing the effect of existing credit-loan balances. Machine-readable CSV and JSON files are accompanied by methodology, source-policy, citation, and README documentation. The dataset contains no personal, confidential, or respondent-level information.

4. Scenario method

The scenario method specifies annual income, represents the relevant DSR ceiling and stress-rate assumptions, incorporates debt service attributable to existing credit debt, and translates the remaining debt-service capacity into an estimated mortgage principal under the stated repayment assumptions.

5. Economic interpretation

Stress DSR links practical borrowing capacity to income and debt-service obligations rather than to property value alone. Two households considering the same-priced dwelling can therefore face materially different borrowing capacity when their incomes or existing liabilities differ. Standardized scenarios can support analysis of housing affordability, household leverage, credit constraints, and policy transmission.

6. Limitations

The calculations are not forecasts, guarantees, or individualized financial advice. Regulatory rules can change, and implementation can differ across loan products and institutions. Users should verify current official rules and lender-specific conditions before making financial decisions.
